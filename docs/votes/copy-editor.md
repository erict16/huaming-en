# Copy editor

Date: 2026-08-19  
Role: Copy editor  
Independent. Plans only.

Test: after ten seconds, can a second-language engineer say what the first screen is.

Live home is the anti-pattern: "A World-wide Presence of Huaming's Technology and Innovation," then a solar slide. About-intro is the same machine voice. Chairman letter still winds up through Edison and Tesla; cut that later.

## Ranked top 3

1. **Plan 05 — Explainer-first**  
   Two short sentences. The device gets a name. On-load vs off-circuit is in the sub. No decoding. This is the only hero an L2 engineer can teach back, word for word.

2. **Plan 01 — Utility handbook**  
   Product, then variants, then PDF and lead time. Catalogue English. They already have these words. They can recite the offer.

3. **Plan 02 — Industrial editorial**  
   "The switch inside the transformer" is a sentence you can repeat. Sub is clean. The 150-country line is a claim, not a definition, but it does not block the explain.

03 is a search box, not a sentence. Fine for someone who already knows CM2. Weak if the job is "explain this screen." 06 leads with an address. Location is not the product.

## Veto

**Plan 04 — Field story.**

Buyer bounce, and the hero is brochure-speak. Three place-names, no product noun. Same move as the live worldwide-presence slide: impress first, define later. A buyer who needs CV2 will not wait for the caption.

## #1 hero rewrite

Plan 05, in my words (2+1):

**Headline:** A tap changer is the moving switch inside a transformer. On-load types work while the transformer is live; off-circuit types only when it is off.

**Sub:** Huaming makes both, vacuum and oil, plus the motor drive.

## Foam

- **04** "Longdong ±800 kV. North Sea platforms. 150 countries." Prestige list. Real jobs, still a campaign line. Covered by the veto.
- **06** "International headquarters, Singapore. Factory, Shanghai." No brochure adjectives, but the first line sells the office. Buyer came for a type.
- **02** "Used in 150+ countries" is a number. Keep it off the headline. The headline itself is fine.
- **03** "the same technical data the factory uses" is a small trust flex. Harmless. Headline is clean.
- **01** and **05** have none.

Do not bring back: worldwide presence, global leader, comprehensive energy solutions, compelling array of reasons, one-stop destination, unwavering commitment.
